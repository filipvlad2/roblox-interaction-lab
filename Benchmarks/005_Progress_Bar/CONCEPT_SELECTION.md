# Progress Bar — Concept Selection

**Stage:** Concept Selection. Reviews all eight philosophies from
`CREATIVE_EXPLORATION.md`, evaluated against `PROGRESS_MODEL.md`'s five
dimensions (Boundedness, Endpoint visibility, Directionality, Basis,
Ownership) and its honesty/trust/tempo/incompatibility rules, then
narrows eight to five. Still philosophy only — no visuals, no animation,
no Roblox, no implementation anywhere below. That work belongs to
`VISUAL_CONCEPTS.md`, which this document exists to feed.

This is a harder evaluation than a plain ranking, because
`PROGRESS_MODEL.md` doesn't just ask "is this a good idea" — it asks
whether a philosophy's honesty obligations are things this Lab can
actually keep, given that every benchmark here is a single-player
session with no real population, no real long-term account, and no real
skill system underneath it. That constraint does real work below,
exactly the way it did when Notification rejected Delight for a
structurally similar reason: not because the philosophy is wrong, but
because this Lab specifically has no way to test the thing its success
actually depends on.

## 1. Ranking

All eight, ordered by overall strength as a candidate to carry forward.
The line falls between 5 and 6 — everything below it is rejected, with
reasoning in Section 2.

1. **Momentum** — the broadest and safest of the eight: it makes no
   promise it can fail to keep, since an unbounded, motion-only
   philosophy has no endpoint to lie about.
2. **Reassurance** — the most purely honest by construction; its entire
   correctness is self-contained and doesn't depend on anything outside
   the interaction itself.
3. **Milestone** — genuinely strong motivational structure, with one
   real, containable honesty risk (arbitrary checkpoints) rather than a
   structural one.
4. **Grind** — powerful and durable when it lands, but the highest-stakes
   of the finalists, entirely dependent on the player's expectations
   being set correctly first.
5. **Threshold** — the cleanest, most honest philosophy of the eight by
   its own design, ranked last among the finalists only because it's
   also the least emotionally ambitious.
6. **Anticipation** — a real, strong idea, rejected for a structural
   honesty exposure `PROGRESS_MODEL.md` surfaces sharply, and because it
   erodes into Momentum under exactly the repetition this Lab's
   benchmarks require.
7. **Mastery** — rejected less on its own merits than on what this Lab
   specifically cannot verify, plus a real, un-ignorable overlap with
   Grind.
8. **Collective** — rejected most fundamentally: its defining variable
   is other real people, which a single-player benchmark session can
   never honestly provide, the same class of problem that rejected
   Delight in Notification's own Concept Selection.

## 2. Rejected philosophies

### Anticipation

- **Strengths:** unmatched at making a wait feel earned rather than
  dead time, when the thing being waited for genuinely deserves it.
- **Weaknesses:** borrows its entire emotional weight from a future
  payoff it doesn't control — a disappointing payoff damages the
  anticipation retroactively, not just the moment itself.
- **Honesty:** the single most exposed philosophy in the set against
  `PROGRESS_MODEL.md`'s "manufactured pacing" trust violation.
  Anticipation's entire mechanism is inducing tension through pacing,
  which is legitimate only when that pacing honestly reflects a real
  wait — and the temptation to add pacing that doesn't correspond to
  anything real is built directly into what makes this philosophy work
  at all. No other philosophy here sits this close to its own worst
  failure mode by default.
- **Versatility:** narrow — needs a specific, known, sufficiently
  significant payoff event to attach to; most of this Lab's benchmark
  interactions don't have one.
- **Repetition tolerance:** low, and for a self-undermining reason —
  `CREATIVE_EXPLORATION.md` already flagged that repeated anticipation
  for the same event quietly converts this philosophy into Momentum
  without anyone deciding that on purpose. A philosophy that degrades
  into a different philosophy under the exact condition (repetition)
  every Lab benchmark needs to survive is a poor foundation to build on.
- **Motivational quality:** high in the moment, but fragile — it's
  borrowed motivation, not built-in.
- **Compatibility with different kinds of progress:** requires Bounded,
  Disclosed, and — critically — genuinely Process-backed progress to
  stay honest; applied to Abstracted progress, its pacing stops being a
  factual claim and becomes pure manufactured drama, which is exactly
  what the honesty risk above describes.
- **Overlap with other philosophies:** collapses into Momentum under
  repetition (noted above), and shares its destination-focus with
  Threshold without either being redundant — but that shared territory
  is exactly where Anticipation's honesty risk lives and Threshold's
  doesn't.
- **Verdict:** rejected. This is now the second benchmark in this Lab
  where Anticipation has been rejected, for closely related reasons —
  structural mismatch and narrow applicability in Notification, and
  here a sharp honesty exposure plus a tendency to erode into a
  different philosophy under repetition. The underlying idea remains
  legitimate; it is specifically fragile in ways this Lab's format keeps
  exposing.

### Mastery

- **Strengths:** builds a durable, personal sense of investment that
  survives long after any single reward would have faded.
- **Weaknesses:** requires the underlying system to genuinely reflect
  skill or effort, not random chance or pure time spent — and that
  requirement is enforced entirely outside whatever the progress
  interaction itself can control.
- **Honesty:** this is Mastery's real problem under
  `PROGRESS_MODEL.md`. Every other philosophy's honesty obligation is
  something the interaction itself can keep or break — Reassurance's
  rate, Threshold's binary claim, Grind's disclosed scale. Mastery's
  central claim ("this reflects real growth") depends on a truth the
  interaction has no way to verify or guarantee on its own. An
  interaction can be built honestly and still end up making a dishonest
  claim, if the system feeding it isn't actually measuring skill — a
  failure mode structurally outside this philosophy's own control.
- **Versatility:** narrow — `CREATIVE_EXPLORATION.md` already noted it
  means nothing in an isolated, single-use context, and this Lab's
  benchmark sessions are exactly that.
- **Repetition tolerance:** high over long timeframes, but that's not
  testable inside a single Lab session either — the property that makes
  Mastery valuable is the one this Lab is least equipped to observe.
- **Motivational quality:** genuinely strong, when real.
- **Compatibility with different kinds of progress:** Persistent-personal
  by definition, which this Lab's stations don't actually have — every
  station resets on Mount, with no real cross-session identity for a
  Mastery bar to accumulate against.
- **Overlap with other philosophies:** substantial overlap with Grind —
  both are long-horizon, Abstracted, and built on investment over time.
  The genuine difference (Grind claims effort, Mastery claims skill) is
  real but subtle enough that a poorly built example of one would likely
  just look like a worse version of the other.
- **Verdict:** rejected — for this Lab specifically, and for this
  benchmark's set, not as a judgment on the underlying idea. It asks for
  two things (a persistent identity, a genuinely skill-reflecting
  system) this Lab's format doesn't have, and where it does apply, it
  competes directly with Grind for the same finalist slot.

### Collective

- **Strengths:** unmatched at building a sense of belonging to something
  larger than an individual session, and the only philosophy of the
  eight built around the Ownership dimension's third value.
- **Weaknesses:** entirely dependent on population and participation it
  can't guarantee on its own — `CREATIVE_EXPLORATION.md` already
  described what happens without that: collapse into a discouraging,
  static number nobody is moving.
- **Honesty:** Collective's core trust risk is exactly the
  "ambiguous ownership" violation `PROGRESS_MODEL.md` names directly —
  and the deeper problem is that a genuinely honest Collective bar
  requires *real other contributors* to exist, which is precisely what
  a single-player benchmark session cannot provide. Any Collective
  example built here would have to fake the "collective" part, which
  makes it dishonest by the very definition this philosophy depends on.
- **Versatility:** narrow by design — needs a populated, socially
  engaged context to mean anything at all.
- **Repetition tolerance:** high across different real events, low for
  any single instance that visibly stalls — and a solo Lab demo of this
  philosophy would be a permanently stalled instance.
- **Motivational quality:** potentially very high, in the right context
  — unreachable in this one.
- **Compatibility with different kinds of progress:** Collective by
  definition, which is the one Ownership value this Lab's single-player
  format structurally cannot honestly produce.
- **Overlap with other philosophies:** minimal in mechanism, but shares
  Bounded/Abstracted territory with Grind and Milestone — the real
  differentiator is Ownership alone, which is exactly the dimension this
  Lab can't test.
- **Verdict:** rejected, on the same grounds Notification rejected
  Delight — not a weak idea, but the one philosophy in this set whose
  success is dominated by a variable (real other players) this Lab has
  no way to provide. Legitimate for a real, populated game, on a
  different day.

## 3. Finalists

### Momentum

- **Strengths:** sustains engagement moment to moment, works with
  almost any activity that's already repetitive, and is structurally
  incapable of the endpoint-related dishonesty every other philosophy
  has to actively guard against.
- **Weaknesses:** struggles to deliver a memorable completion, because
  the destination was never the point.
- **Honesty:** the lowest-risk philosophy in the set under
  `PROGRESS_MODEL.md` — because it typically has no disclosed endpoint,
  most of the model's honesty obligations (goalposts, precision,
  disclosed-endpoint delivery) simply don't apply to it. Its one real
  obligation — that the visible motion is real, not manufactured — is
  also the simplest of any philosophy here to keep.
- **Versatility:** the highest of the five finalists; applies to nearly
  any ongoing, repeated activity regardless of genre.
- **Repetition tolerance:** very high, provided the motion stays honest
  and perceptible.
- **Motivational quality:** good at sustaining continued action, weak at
  driving toward a specific goal.
- **Compatibility with different kinds of progress:** naturally
  Unbounded and Concealed — this is the finalist set's representative
  for that entire half of the Boundedness dimension, which none of the
  other four finalists cover.
- **Overlap with other philosophies:** shares "the process itself is the
  point" territory with Reassurance, but differs in stakes — Reassurance
  demands nothing, Momentum is meant to actively feel good to watch.
- **Why it deserves visual exploration:** it's the toolkit's only
  Unbounded representative and its safest, most versatile entry —
  without it, the finalist set would only ever be able to represent
  progress that has an ending, which most ongoing play doesn't.

### Milestone

- **Strengths:** turns a long task into several short ones
  psychologically, and gives natural, honest points to stop and resume.
- **Weaknesses:** unevenly spaced or arbitrary checkpoints actively hurt
  more than a smooth continuum would.
- **Honesty:** Milestone's honesty risk maps directly onto
  `PROGRESS_MODEL.md`'s "comparable causes produce comparable visible
  effects" principle — its checkpoints have to represent real, roughly
  equivalent divisions of the underlying task, or the structure itself
  becomes the lie. This is real but containable: unlike Anticipation's
  risk, which is baked into the philosophy's core mechanism, Milestone's
  risk is a matter of correctly authoring the checkpoints.
- **Versatility:** strong across any structured content with genuine
  sub-steps.
- **Repetition tolerance:** high for the structure itself; each
  individual checkpoint's payoff fatigues the way any frequent small
  confirmation does.
- **Motivational quality:** strong — concrete, achievable sub-goals are
  one of the most reliable motivators in the whole set.
- **Compatibility with different kinds of progress:** typically Bounded
  and Disclosed at each step, and can be built on either Basis
  (Process-backed for timed stages, Abstracted for narrative or skill
  stages) — the most flexible finalist on the Basis dimension.
- **Overlap with other philosophies:** real and worth naming — Milestone
  is often literally how a Grind gets presented internally. The
  distinction that keeps them separate: Milestone's promise is that each
  step matters on its own; Grind's promise is that the totality matters
  because of its size. A Milestone-structured Grind still needs Grind's
  own scale-honesty rule satisfied independently.
- **Why it deserves visual exploration:** it's the finalist set's
  clearest demonstration of turning size into approachability without
  sacrificing honesty, and the only one built around discrete,
  individually meaningful steps rather than a continuum.

### Reassurance

- **Strengths:** cheap to deliver real trust for very little emotional
  investment, and good at exactly the one thing it tries to do.
- **Weaknesses:** carries essentially zero emotional payload of its own;
  any attempt to make it exciting is a misunderstanding of the
  philosophy.
- **Honesty:** the strictest and simplest honesty case in the set —
  `PROGRESS_MODEL.md`'s process-backed rule applies here in its purest
  form, since Reassurance exists specifically to represent a real
  operation. There's no ambiguity about what standard it's held to.
- **Versatility:** broad but shallow — usable anywhere something needs
  to be shown as alive, never the philosophy carrying an interaction's
  main emotional weight.
- **Repetition tolerance:** effectively unlimited, provided it never
  overstates its own importance.
- **Motivational quality:** essentially none by design — it isn't trying
  to motivate, only to reassure.
- **Compatibility with different kinds of progress:** the finalist set's
  clearest, most literal Process-backed, Bounded-or-easily-inferable,
  Individual example — the baseline every other finalist can be
  compared against.
- **Overlap with other philosophies:** shares "process over destination"
  territory with Momentum, differentiated entirely by stakes and by how
  strictly its honesty is held.
- **Why it deserves visual exploration:** every other finalist needs
  Reassurance's honesty standard as a reference point, and this Lab's
  own stations will likely need a plain, low-ambition "something is
  happening" mode regardless of which other philosophy is doing the
  main emotional work at any given moment.

### Grind

- **Strengths:** produces some of the most durable, meaningful
  completion moments possible, because the payoff is inseparable from
  the cost.
- **Weaknesses:** actively unpleasant if the player didn't expect the
  scale, or didn't choose to be in it.
- **Honesty:** the finalist set's clearest demonstration of
  `PROGRESS_MODEL.md`'s tempo principle that slowness can be a fully
  honest, deliberate choice — because Grind is Abstracted, its pacing
  isn't a factual claim the way a Process-backed bar's would be, so the
  designer has genuine, legitimate latitude over how it feels. That
  latitude comes with one hard condition: the scale communicated has to
  match what the player was actually told to expect, which is a
  communication problem, not a structural one.
- **Versatility:** strong across any long-horizon system the player has
  opted into.
- **Repetition tolerance:** paradoxical — low for watching the same
  presentation repeatedly in one sitting, but the entire philosophy is
  built around tolerating one very long single repetition, a genuinely
  different axis than the other finalists are measured on.
- **Motivational quality:** very strong for players who've bought in;
  actively harmful for players who haven't.
- **Compatibility with different kinds of progress:** Bounded (usually
  eventually), Disclosed, Abstracted — the finalist set's representative
  for legitimate designer discretion over tempo.
- **Overlap with other philosophies:** the real overlap risk here is
  with Mastery, not with any other finalist — see Mastery's rejection
  above for why Grind, not Mastery, is the one that survived that
  overlap.
- **Why it deserves visual exploration:** it's the only finalist whose
  entire value proposition is that deliberate slowness and scale are
  the message, not a cost to be minimized — none of the other four can
  stand in for that.

### Threshold

- **Strengths:** extremely clear and honest about what matters — there's
  no ambiguity about whether partial progress counts for anything.
- **Weaknesses:** the least emotionally rewarding philosophy to simply
  watch, by its own design.
- **Honesty:** the philosophy `PROGRESS_MODEL.md`'s honesty section most
  directly rewards. It never claims partial value it doesn't have,
  which is precisely the "precision matches what's actually known"
  principle in its cleanest possible form — Threshold's entire design is
  built to avoid the exact false-precision failure other philosophies
  have to actively guard against.
- **Versatility:** moderate — needs a genuinely binary condition, which
  is common (cooldowns, hard unlocks) but not universal.
- **Repetition tolerance:** low — watching a gate you can't influence
  the speed of is tolerable occasionally and grating if it happens
  often.
- **Motivational quality:** low in the moment, but structurally honest
  about that rather than pretending otherwise.
- **Compatibility with different kinds of progress:** Bounded by
  definition, and can be either Disclosed or Concealed, Process-backed
  (cooldowns) or Abstracted (hidden gates) — narrower in emotional range
  than the other finalists but flexible on the dimensions that don't
  drive its emotional register.
- **Overlap with other philosophies:** shares destination-focus with
  Anticipation without sharing its honesty exposure — Threshold never
  claims an event is coming, only that a gate exists, which is the exact
  distinction that let it survive Concept Selection where Anticipation
  didn't.
- **Why it deserves visual exploration:** it's the finalist set's
  cleanest positive proof that low ambition and strict honesty can be a
  deliberate, correct design choice rather than a missing feature — a
  necessary counterweight to Grind and Milestone's higher emotional
  ambition.

## 4. Toolkit rationale

These five don't just avoid being redundant with each other — together
they span every axis `PROGRESS_MODEL.md` actually defined, in a way no
smaller subset could:

- **Boundedness:** Momentum is the toolkit's only Unbounded
  representative; Milestone, Reassurance, Grind, and Threshold are all
  Bounded. Between them, the toolkit covers both sides of the single
  most consequential dimension the model identified.
- **Basis and honesty regime:** Reassurance and Threshold are the
  toolkit's strict Process-backed-or-binary honesty anchors, where the
  interaction's correctness is fully self-contained. Grind is the
  toolkit's deliberate Abstracted case, where honesty depends on
  communicated expectations rather than a factual rate. Milestone can be
  either, depending on what it's structuring. Together, the toolkit
  demonstrates both honesty regimes `PROGRESS_MODEL.md` defined, not
  just one of them.
- **Tempo:** Reassurance and Momentum are the model's "fast is honest"
  cases — repetition-heavy, process-or-motion-driven. Grind and
  Threshold are its "slow is honest" cases — weight and gating,
  respectively. The toolkit spans the model's entire tempo argument in
  both directions, each backed by a real finalist rather than an
  abstract example.
- **Emotional register:** Momentum is ambient and ongoing, Milestone is
  incremental achievement, Reassurance is calm utility, Grind is weighty
  investment, and Threshold is honest restraint. Five genuinely
  different jobs, with the overlaps that did exist (Anticipation into
  Momentum, Mastery into Grind, Collective's ownership axis this Lab
  can't test) already resolved by rejection rather than left
  unaddressed.
- **Testability:** every one of the five can be honestly built and
  demonstrated inside a single-player Lab benchmark session without
  requiring anything the Lab structurally lacks — a real population, a
  persistent cross-session identity, or a genuine external skill system.
  That's not true of any of the three rejected philosophies, and it's
  as much a property of this toolkit as any of the design dimensions
  above.

A benchmark built using only one of these five would be missing
something real: all-Momentum and nothing ever feels finished; all-Grind
and nothing feels approachable; all-Reassurance and nothing feels
motivating; all-Threshold and nothing along the way feels rewarding;
all-Milestone and nothing represents genuinely continuous processes
honestly. The five together cover both ends of Boundedness, both of the
model's honesty regimes, both legitimate tempo directions, and five
non-overlapping emotional jobs — that completeness, not any individual
philosophy's strength, is the actual argument for this set of five over
any other combination of eight choose five.
